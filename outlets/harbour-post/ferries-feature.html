Seventy years on the water: the story of Lantern Bay Ferries

By Owen Pell, local history

Lantern Bay Ferries began in 1956 with one wooden boat, the Kittiwake, and a ticket office in a fisherman's hut on the quay. Ada and Ned Orrin carried post, sheep and the occasional doctor across to Gull Island, and on a calm day the crossing took a little under an hour.

The company bought its first car ferry in 1974, and the slipway on the island was rebuilt to take it. The old hut gave way to the brick office that still stands at the end of Quay Street, and the Kittiwake was sold to a museum on the mainland, where she can be seen today.

Three generations of the Orrin family have run the company since. Its two boats, the Puffin and the Shearwater, now make four crossings to Gull Island each day, and the timetable has hardly changed in years. Ask anyone on the quay and they will tell you the first boat of the morning has not missed a day for weather since the great storm of 1987.
